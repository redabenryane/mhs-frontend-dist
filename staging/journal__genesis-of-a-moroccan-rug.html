From Wool to Rug

3 min read

A Moroccan rug begins long before the first knot. From wool preparation to weaving, washing and finishing, each stage is shaped by hand, material and time.

It Starts With the Wool

Every handmade Moroccan rug begins with wool. Its quality, softness and natural character influence how the finished rug will look, feel and age over time.

The wool is carefully prepared before weaving begins, creating the foundation for the rug’s texture, density and distinctive feel. As a natural material, wool carries subtle variations in tone and texture that become part of the character of each piece.

Preparing the Design

Before the first knot is tied, the dimensions, composition and colors of the rug are established.

For our made-to-order and bespoke Moroccan rugs, these decisions define the proportions and visual rhythm of the finished piece. The design becomes a guide for the artisan, translating an idea into a rug made entirely by hand in Morocco.

Knot by Knot

A hand-knotted Moroccan rug takes shape gradually on the loom, one knot at a time.

This is the most time-intensive part of the process. Depending on the rug’s dimensions, construction and design complexity, weaving can require weeks or months of skilled work.

Small variations in tension, texture and detail are inherent to traditional handcraft and give every rug its individual character.

Washing & Finishing

Once the weaving is complete, the Moroccan rug moves through washing and finishing.

These final stages reveal the natural texture of the wool, soften the surface and bring depth to its colors. The pile and edges are carefully finished before the rug is prepared to leave Morocco for its final destination.

From Morocco to Your Interior

What begins as raw wool becomes a piece designed to live in an interior for years to come.

Every Moroccan Heritage Studio rug carries traces of how it was made: the natural wool, the hand of the maker and the time required to create it.

From raw wool to the final finish, every stage is carried out by hand in Morocco.
